The Research Behind ANGI

Adapting to the Learner

Learning can be more effective when teaching adapts to what a learner knows.

What the study found

A review of 16 randomized trials found that technology-supported learning approaches that adapted to learners’ levels showed larger effects than less adaptive approaches.

How it informs Inksmore

This work informs our focus on adapting lessons to a child’s current skills, rather than relying on age alone.

Study context

The studies involved learners ages 6 to 15 in five low- and middle-income countries. They do not establish effects for preschool children or Inksmore.

The effectiveness of technology-supported personalised learning in low- and middle-income countries: A meta-analysis

Louis Major, Gill A. Francis & Maria Tsapali

British Journal of Educational Technology, 52(5), 1935 to 1964 · 2021

Connecting Knowledge

A connected picture of skills can reveal more than a single score.

What the study found

Knowledge Space Theory represents a learner’s knowledge as a state within a structured collection of possible knowledge states. The framework supports assessment of what is known and identification of possible next learning steps.

How it informs Inksmore

It informs our use of connected skills to make foundations and possible next steps visible in a learning profile.

Study context

This is a theoretical reference, not a validation of ANGI’s implementation.

Knowledge Spaces and Learning Spaces

Jean-Paul Doignon & Jean-Claude Falmagne

Author-posted theoretical overview · arXiv:1511.06757 · 2015

Learning from Every Practice

Each response can help refine the picture of a learner’s understanding.

What the study found

Deep Knowledge Tracing used recurrent neural networks to model learning from sequences of learner interactions, improving predictions of future performance on the datasets studied.

How it informs Inksmore

It informs our approach to updating a learning profile as practice and feedback accumulate, rather than treating an initial assessment as permanent.

Study context

Better prediction does not itself demonstrate improved learning outcomes or validate Inksmore.

Deep Knowledge Tracing

Chris Piech, Jonathan Bassen, Jonathan Huang, Surya Ganguli, Mehran Sahami, Leonidas Guibas & Jascha Sohl-Dickstein

Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 28 · 2015

Why We Learn on Paper

Handwriting & Early Literacy

Writing by hand can support early letter and word learning.

What the study found

In a study of 50 children averaging 5.4 years old, handwriting practice outperformed typing on several tasks involving newly learned letters and words.

How it informs Inksmore

It informs our decision to include opportunities for drawing and writing alongside lesson activities.

Study context

This study concerns early literacy, not math or every form of screen-based learning.

The impact of handwriting and typing practice in children’s letter and word learning: Implications for literacy development

Gorka Ibaibarriaga, Joana Acha & Manuel Perea

Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 253, 106195 · 2025

How Children Form Letters

Forming letters by hand can shape how children process them.

What the study found

Five-year-old prereaders practiced printing, tracing, or typing letters. Brain scans showed that free-form printing engaged a reading-related brain circuit during later letter viewing in ways tracing and typing did not.

How it informs Inksmore

It informs our interest in activities where children actively form marks and letters, alongside other ways of exploring on paper.

Study context

Brain activity differences do not establish higher intelligence or long-term learning gains.

The effects of handwriting experience on functional brain development in pre-literate children

Karin H. James & Laura Engelhardt

Trends in Neuroscience and Education, 1(1), 32 to 42 · 2012

Paper Reading & Comprehension

Paper can support story understanding. Book design matters, too.

What the study found

A review of 39 studies involving 1,812 children ages 1 to 8 found an advantage for paper in story comprehension when books differed mainly by format. Digital enhancements aligned with the story could favor digital books.

How it informs Inksmore

It informs our choice to preserve paper-based reading opportunities while recognizing that content and design also matter.

Study context

These findings concern story reading, not all subjects or all uses of screens.

A Comparison of Children’s Reading on Paper Versus Screen: A Meta-Analysis

May Irene Furenes, Natalia Kucirkova & Adriana G. Bus

Review of Educational Research, 91(4), 483 to 517 · 2021

How to read this result

The figure compares the improvement in scores between the two groups. It does not mean that final scores were 70% higher, or that scores increased by 70 percentage points.
